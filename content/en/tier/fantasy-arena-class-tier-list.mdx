## S-Tier Breakdown: The Meta Definers

### Mage — The Pylon Powerhouse

The Mage has emerged as the surprise king of the current meta, and community reports suggest it might be the most cost-effective class in the entire game. Despite being only a Rare-tier class, its pylon tower strategy rivals even the most expensive Mythic options.

Here's why the Mage dominates:

- **Pylon towers have insane range** for a low-cost class
- **Enemies get trapped on map ramps**, giving towers extended kill time
- **Spawn killing becomes possible** once you've placed enough upgraded pylons
- **Works solo or in groups**—co-op makes it nearly unstoppable
- **Affordable to purchase outright**, saving your class slots for rolling other options

The core strategy involves saving up your burgers early, then clustering pylon towers near the ramp area where enemies path inefficiently. As you accumulate more towers and upgrade them to max level, they begin eliminating enemies the moment they spawn—assuming you keep auto skip turned off.

| Mage Strategy Step | Action | Priority |
|---------------------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Buy Mage from class shop | High |
| 2 | Equip best available weapon | Medium |
| 3 | Redeem active codes for bonus burgers | High |
| 4 | Enter match and save resources early | Critical |
| 5 | Place pylons near the ramp chokepoint | Critical |
| 6 | Continuously add and upgrade towers | Critical |
| 7 | Turn off auto skip for spawn kills | High |

Player experience indicates the Mage is almost certainly due for a nerf given how dramatically it outperforms its rarity tier. If you haven't unlocked it yet, prioritize doing so immediately.

### Shadow Blade — The Premium Carry

The Mythic-class Shadow Blade sits at the top alongside the Mage for entirely different reasons. Where the Mage excels through tactical tower placement, Shadow Blade delivers devastating burst damage through its stealth mechanics. It's the most expensive class in the game, but player experience confirms it can solo carry runs when piloted correctly.

## A-Tier Classes: Strong and Reliable

The A-Tier contains classes that consistently perform well without reaching the dominant heights of S-Tier. These are excellent investments if you already have the S-Tier options or prefer different playstyles.

**Paladin** offers incredible survivability through its Holy Shield ability, making it perfect for players who prefer a methodical approach. The shield absorbs massive damage, buying time for teammates or turrets to deal with threats.

**Ranger** provides consistent ranged damage with its Multi-Shot ability. While it lacks the Mage's tower strategy, it's more flexible across different map positions and doesn't require specific chokepoint knowledge.

**Necromancer** summons a horde of minions that can overwhelm enemies through sheer numbers. It scales exceptionally well into later waves but has a slower start compared to other A-Tier options.

## B-Tier and Below: Niche but Not Useless

Classes in the B-Tier and lower aren't necessarily bad—they simply require more effort, specific team compositions, or haven't received the balance attention they need.

**Berserker** and **Frost Mage** both have dedicated player bases who swear by them. The Berserker's Rage Mode can delete bosses when timed correctly, while Frost Mage's freeze zones provide valuable crowd control. The Engineer's turrets share some conceptual space with the Mage's pylons but lack the range and scaling.

The Common-tier Warrior and Scout are perfectly serviceable for learning the game, but you'll want to upgrade quickly. The Healer, unfortunately, suffers from Fantasy Arena's current design where damage output matters more than sustain in endless mode.
